# Daily Loss Limit and Pre-Trade Checklist for Options Day Traders: Worked Example (free)

Arithmetic and process only. Not advice, not a signal. The account size and trades below are made-up numbers and say nothing about what any strategy earns. Options involve risk of loss, and you can lose your whole premium.

## Why a daily loss limit

A per-trade risk rule limits one bad trade. A daily loss limit limits a bad day, when losses tend to bunch together. Decide both before the open, write them down, and stop when the limit is hit.

## Worked example (made-up $5,000 account)

- Risk per trade (1R) = 1% of account = **$50**
- Daily loss limit = 3R = **$150**, which is **3.0%** of the account
- After three full -1R losses the balance is $4,850; the next 1R would be 1% of $4,850 = **$48.50** (risk shrinks as the account shrinks)
- Contracts for a defined-risk trade: a 1-wide call debit spread bought for $0.40 has a max loss of $0.40 x 100 = **$40 per spread**, so $50 / $40 = 1.25, rounded **down** to **1 spread** (max loss $40, under the $50 limit). Never round up.
- Long options and debit spreads: max loss is the debit paid plus fees. For credit spreads, max loss = (width - credit) x 100 per spread.

## Pre-trade checklist (answer before every order)

1. Is this setup in my written plan? (If not, no trade.)
2. What is my max loss in dollars, and is it at or under 1R?
3. Where is my exit if I am wrong, and have I counted fees and slippage?
4. Have I hit today's loss limit or max trade count? (If yes, stop for the day.)
5. Am I trading to follow the plan, or to win back a loss?
6. Is there scheduled news (CPI, FOMC, big earnings) that my plan says to sit out?

## After the close

Log the trade and its R-multiple, note whether you followed the plan, and write one line on what you would repeat or change.

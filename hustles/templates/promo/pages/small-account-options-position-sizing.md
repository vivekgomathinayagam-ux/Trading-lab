# Small Account Options Position Sizing: How Many Contracts Fit? (free table)

Arithmetic only. Not advice, not a signal, no prediction of results. Options involve risk of loss, and you can lose your whole premium. Account sizes, risk percentages and contract costs below are made-up round numbers. Written with AI assistance (Claude); every cell recomputed by script.

## The rule

**Contracts = round down( account x risk % / max loss of ONE contract )**

A result of **0 means the trade is too big for your rule**. Skip it or choose a cheaper structure. Do not bend the rule.

## What the table shows

- On a $1,000 account with a 5% rule, a $50-max-loss spread fits **once**; a $100 one does not fit at all.
- Cutting the risk rule to 1% on small accounts leaves almost every trade at 0. That is a signal to use a tighter structure or paper trade until the account grows, not to raise the percentage.
- Remember to add commissions to max loss (see the break-even-with-fees page) before rounding down.
- Never sell naked options: their loss has no cap, so no table can size them.

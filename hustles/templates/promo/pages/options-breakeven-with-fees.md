# Options Break-Even With Commissions: Worked Examples (free)

Arithmetic only. Not advice, not a signal. Prices, strikes and the $0.65 per-contract fee are made-up round numbers; check your broker's real fees. Options involve risk of loss, and you can lose your whole premium.

## The rule

Fees move your break-even. Per-share fee impact = (total fees for all contracts and sides) / (100 x contracts).

## Example 1: long call (2 contracts)

- Strike 500, premium $2.50, fee $0.65 per contract per side, held to a closing sale.
- Premium paid: 2.50 x 100 x 2 = **$500.00**. Round-trip fees: 0.65 x 2 contracts x 2 sides = **$2.60**. Total at risk: **$502.60**.
- Fee impact per share: 2.60 / 200 = **$0.013**. Break-even at close of the trade: 500 + 2.50 + 0.013 = **$502.513**.

## Example 2: call debit spread, 500/501, 1 contract

- Debit $0.40, fee $0.65 per contract per leg per side: 2 legs x 2 sides x 0.65 = **$2.60**.
- Max loss: 40.00 + 2.60 = **$42.60**. Max profit: (100 - 40.00) - 2.60 = **$57.40**.
- Break-even: 500 + 0.40 + 0.026 = **$500.426**.

## Example 3: put credit spread, 500/499, 1 contract, expires worthless or is assigned out

- Credit $0.35; opening fees only: 2 legs x 0.65 = **$1.30**.
- Max profit: 35.00 - 1.30 = **$33.70**. Max loss: (100 - 35.00) + 1.30 = **$66.30**.
- Break-even: 500 - 0.35 + 0.013 = **$499.663**.

## Takeaways

- On cheap spreads, fees are a large share of the max profit (Example 2: 2.60 of 60.00 is 4.3%).
- Size from max loss **including** fees, and round contracts down.
- Check any plan against a sheet before the order, not after.
